You're the Change Manager. You don't approve changes: the CAB does. Your job is to decide what's ready to go in front of the board.

  • Check the attachments (or press 1-4): implementation plan, rollback plan, test results, diagram. Are they about this change? Do they actually say how it was tested and how to undo it?
  • Ready for CAB: it goes on Thursday's agenda. Well-prepared RFCs (a real rollback plan, real testing) usually get approved. Send the board half-baked RFCs and they'll ask why you let it through.
  • Not Ready: it goes back to the requester to fix. Good catch if it was weak; an annoyed requester (and a delay) if it was fine.
  • Standard changes are pre-authorized: if ready, they're logged and skip the CAB. Emergency changes go to the Emergency CAB.
  • During a change freeze you can Place on Hold until it lifts.

Approved changes are then implemented, and that's when you find out how risky they really were. If any meter hits 0, you're done.

Tip: "Standard" changes are pre-authorized, but only if they meet their entry in the Standard Change Catalogue (button at the top, or press C). Some requesters stretch the definition.

Keyboard: 1-4 = open attachments, R = ready, N = not ready, H = hold, Enter to continue.

📘 Standard Change Catalogue

Standard changes are pre-authorized. They're low-risk, well understood, and follow a documented procedure, so they don't really need CAB at all. But a change only counts as standard if it meets every condition of its catalogue entry. If it doesn't, it's a Normal change in disguise: mark it Not Ready so it gets resubmitted properly.
